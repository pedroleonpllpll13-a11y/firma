# Genre calibration

The six audits do not apply with equal force everywhere. A property listing must state
its facts; a reel script does not need a moral. Calibrate before rewriting. The
short-text rule: under ~400 words, focus on audits 1 (theme), 3 (emotion mode), 4
(specificity), and 6 (shape convergence). Tangents and time jumps need room.

Adapted from humanizer-stack's `genre-calibration.md` (MIT) for real-estate and
personal-brand content in English and Spanish.

## Instagram captions and carousels

Short, and the genre already allows direct address and a takeaway, so the tell is
uniformity: every caption shaped hook -> story -> turning point -> stated insight ->
"guarda este post".

- Insight stated at most once. About a third of captions should not state it at all;
  end on the moment, the number, or the detail and let comments interpret.
- Rotate skeletons: outcome first ("Vendido en 11 días."), mid-scene open, delayed
  number, unstated lesson. Never the same shape twice in a row.
- Named specifics are the cheapest human marker: the street or urbanización, the
  price or price per m², the days on market, the real objection the buyer raised.
  Only with the client's permission and only real figures.
- Plain emotion: "me dio pena dejar ir esa casa" beats "se me encogió el corazón".
- Skip: fourth-wall moves beyond one light touch.

## Reel, YouTube, and podcast scripts

Spoken text makes AI shape easy to hear: even pacing, a lesson every 30 seconds, and a
tidy wrap-up.

- Vary intensity. Flat, even energy is the Claude fingerprint; let one beat spike.
- Cold open mid-scene or on the result, then rewind.
- Delayed reveal works well on video: hold the price, the outcome, or the twist until
  two-thirds in.
- End hot. Cut the "so, in summary" coda after the natural ending.
- Plain emotion and named places. "Tenía miedo de que la tasación saliera baja" beats
  any description of a tight chest.
- Skip: heavy nonlinearity in reels under 60 seconds.

## Property descriptions and listings

Both brand guides ask for the life before the square metres, so keep that order. The
tell is a life built from generic atmosphere instead of this house. The structural
tells here are the stated lesson ("para quienes entienden el valor de…"), the setting
painted as a mood ("el silencio, la luz y la amplitud te dicen todo"), and vagueness.

- Build the life from facts of this property: where the light comes in and when, what
  the terrace looks at, the minutes to the named club or school, the real routine the
  layout allows ("la luz entra por el este toda la mañana y la piscina queda siempre a
  la vista desde el salón").
- One sensory detail, and only one that is true of this house ("se oye el mar desde el
  dormitorio principal"). Drop the rest of the atmosphere.
- No lifestyle moral at the end. Close on a fact or the next step ("Visitas con cita
  previa.").
- Skip: tangents, time jumps, reader-address gimmicks. Pass 1 matters more here.

## Client emails and WhatsApp messages

Intimate, short, and read on a phone. Body-performance emotion and restated lessons
read especially fake at this distance.

- Lead with the decision or the news. Background only if the client lacks it
  (humanizer §26).
- Lesson or recommendation once, not at the top and again at the bottom.
- Name the feeling when there is one: "entiendo que es frustrante".
- Named specifics over category words: the notary's name, the date, the amount.
- Skip: nonlinear structure, tangents.

## Newsletters, blog posts, dossiers

Full menu available. This is where structural choices pay most.

- One deliberate structural choice per piece: nonlinear open, recontextualization
  callback, oblique tangent, or open thread. Pick per piece, vary across pieces.
- No epilogue coda. When the piece lands, stop.
- Ambivalent closes are allowed where genuine: a market that is good for sellers and
  hard for buyers can stay both.
- In a newsletter, the PS is a natural slot for an oblique tangent; use it as one,
  not as a second call to action.
- A dossier for a specific client is closer to an email: facts and the client's own
  needs, no invented narrative.

## Everything, always

- Audit 6 (shape convergence) applies to every genre: check the skeleton against the
  last 2-3 pieces in the same channel before publishing.
- Never deploy the full intervention menu in one piece. 1-2 moves, chosen on purpose.
- Brand voice rules (the voice layer) win on word choice and tone.
